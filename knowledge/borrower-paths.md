# Home Buying Paths for Different Borrowers

> **DISCLAIMER**: This is educational guidance only. Loan eligibility depends on individual circumstances. Consult with a licensed loan officer for personalized advice.

## General Consumer Path (Conventional Loans)

### Overview
- Conforming loans backed by Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac
- Typical credit score requirement: 620+ (ideal: 740+)
- Down payment: 3-20% (PMI required below 20%)
- DTI ratio: Generally up to 45% (can be higher with compensating factors)

### Steps
1. **Pre-qualification**: Initial assessment of affordability
2. **Pre-approval**: Full credit pull, income/asset verification
3. **Home search**: Work with real estate agent
4. **Offer and contract**: Make offer, negotiate terms
5. **Loan application**: Complete 1003, provide documentation
6. **Processing**: Verification of information
7. **Underwriting**: Final loan decision
8. **Closing**: Sign documents, fund loan, transfer title

### Required Documentation
- Photo ID (driver's license, passport)
- Social Security number
- Pay stubs (most recent 30 days)
- W-2s (2 years)
- Tax returns (2 years) if self-employed or complex income
- Bank statements (2 months)
- Gift letters if applicable
- Explanation letters for credit issues

## Veteran Path (VA Loans)

### Eligibility
- Active duty service members
- Veterans with honorable discharge
- National Guard and Reserve members (conditions apply)
- Surviving spouses (conditions apply)

### Benefits
- **Zero down payment**: 100% financing available
- **No PMI**: Significant monthly savings
- **Competitive rates**: Often lower than conventional
- **Limited closing costs**: VA caps what veterans pay
- **Easier qualification**: More flexible DTI and credit

### Requirements
- Certificate of Eligibility (COE) from VA
- Meet lender's credit and income requirements (no VA minimum credit score)
- Property must be primary residence
- Must meet VA minimum property requirements (MPRs)
- VA funding fee (can be financed, waived for disabled veterans)

### VA Loan Types
- Purchase loans
- Interest Rate Reduction Refinance Loan (IRRRL)
- Cash-out refinance
- Native American Direct Loan (NADL)
- Adapted Housing Grants

## Low-Credit Borrower Path (FHA Loans)

### Overview
- Loans insured by Federal Housing Administration
- More lenient credit requirements
- Lower down payment than conventional

### Credit Requirements
- 580+ credit score: 3.5% down payment
- 500-579 credit score: 10% down payment
- Below 500: Generally not eligible

### Key Features
- **Mortgage Insurance**: Upfront MIP (1.75%) + annual MIP (0.55% for most)
- **DTI flexibility**: Up to 43% standard, higher with compensating factors
- **Manual underwriting**: Available for complex cases
- **Gift funds**: 100% of down payment can be a gift

### Considerations
- MIP for life of loan (unless refinanced to conventional)
- Property standards (FHA appraisal requirements)
- Loan limits vary by county
- Primary residence only

## First-Time Homebuyer Programs

### Down Payment Assistance (DPA)
- State and local programs
- Grants or forgivable loans
- Income and purchase price limits apply
- Often combined with FHA or conventional loans

### HomeReady (Fannie Mae)
- 3% down payment
- Income limits (80% AMI in most areas)
- Homeownership education required
- Reduced PMI rates

### Home Possible (Freddie Mac)
- 3% down payment
- Income limits (80% AMI)
- Flexible sources for down payment
- Homeownership education options

### Good Neighbor Next Door (HUD)
- 50% discount on HUD homes
- For teachers, law enforcement, firefighters, EMTs
- Must live in property 3 years

## USDA Rural Development Loans

### Overview
- Zero down payment for rural and suburban areas
- Income limits apply (115% of area median income)
- Property must be in eligible area

### Features
- No down payment required
- Competitive interest rates
- Upfront and annual guarantee fees (lower than FHA MIP)
- Primary residence only

## Credit Repair Considerations

### Timeline
- Significant credit improvement: 6-12 months minimum
- Bankruptcy: 2-4 years depending on type and loan program
- Foreclosure: 2-7 years depending on loan program
- Short sale: 2-4 years depending on loan program

### Quick Wins
- Dispute errors on credit reports
- Pay down credit card balances (utilization under 30%)
- Become authorized user on established account
- Don't close old accounts
- Don't apply for new credit during mortgage process
