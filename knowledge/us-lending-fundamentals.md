# US Lending Industry Fundamentals

## Regulatory Framework

### CFPB (Consumer Financial Protection Bureau)
- Primary federal regulator for consumer financial products
- Enforces TILA, RESPA, ECOA, FCRA, and other consumer protection laws
- Issues regulations (Regulation Z, X, B, V, etc.)
- Supervises large banks, non-banks, and mortgage servicers
- Website: consumerfinance.gov

### HMDA (Home Mortgage Disclosure Act)
- Requires lenders to report mortgage data annually
- Data includes loan applications, originations, purchases
- Used to monitor fair lending compliance
- Key data points: loan amount, property location, applicant demographics, loan purpose, action taken
- HMDA data is publicly available for transparency

### LEI (Legal Entity Identifier)
- 20-character alphanumeric code identifying legal entities in financial transactions
- Required for HMDA reporting
- Obtained through GLEIF-accredited Local Operating Units (LOUs)
- Must be renewed annually

## Federal Reserve and Interest Rates

### Fed Funds Rate
- Target rate set by Federal Open Market Committee (FOMC)
- Check current rate: federalreserve.gov or fred.stlouisfed.org
- Influences mortgage rates indirectly through Treasury yields and MBS pricing

### Key Rate Benchmarks
- SOFR (Secured Overnight Financing Rate) - replaced LIBOR
- Prime Rate - typically Fed Funds + 3%
- Treasury yields (10-year influences mortgage rates)

## Lender Types

### Depositories
- Banks and credit unions that accept deposits
- Subject to OCC, FDIC, or NCUA oversight
- Can hold loans in portfolio or sell to secondary market
- Examples: JPMorgan Chase, Wells Fargo, Bank of America

### Credit Unions
- Member-owned, not-for-profit cooperatives
- Regulated by NCUA (National Credit Union Administration)
- Often offer competitive rates due to tax-exempt status
- Membership requirements vary

### IMBs (Independent Mortgage Banks)
- Non-depository mortgage lenders
- Regulated by state agencies and CFPB
- Cannot hold deposits; rely on warehouse lines
- Often more flexible but may have higher rates
- Examples: Rocket Mortgage, loanDepot, United Wholesale Mortgage

## Loan Origination System (LOS)

### Purpose
- Software platform managing the loan lifecycle
- From application through closing and delivery
- Tracks documents, compliance, pricing, and workflow

### Key Functions
- Application intake and processing
- Document management
- Automated underwriting submission
- Compliance checking (TRID, ATR/QM, fair lending)
- Pricing and lock management
- Closing coordination

### Common LOS Platforms
- Encompass (ICE Mortgage Technology)
- BytePro
- Calyx Point
- MortgageDirector
- Blend (POS with LOS features)

## Point of Sale (POS)

### Purpose
- Borrower-facing application interface
- Digital mortgage application experience
- Collects initial data and documents

### Features
- Online application forms
- Document upload
- Asset/income verification integrations
- Real-time pricing
- E-consent and disclosures
- Mobile-friendly interfaces

### Common POS Platforms

- Maxwell

## Capital Markets

### Secondary Market Overview
- Loans sold to GSEs (Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac), Ginnie Mae, or private investors
- Provides liquidity for continued lending
- Pricing based on MBS market

### Key Concepts
- Lock period: guaranteed rate/price timeframe
- Pair-off: penalty for not delivering locked loan
- Gain-on-sale: profit from selling loans above par
- Pipeline management: balancing locks with deliveries
- Hedging: protecting against rate movements

### GSE Requirements
- Fannie Mae Selling Guide
- Freddie Mac Seller/Servicer Guide
- Conforming loan limits (updated annually)
- Credit, income, and property requirements
