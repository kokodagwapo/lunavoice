# 50-State Lending Regulation Overview

> **DISCLAIMER**: This is a high-level summary for educational purposes. State laws change frequently. Always verify current requirements with state regulators and legal counsel before operating in any state.

## Licensing Requirements

### NMLS (Nationwide Multistate Licensing System)
- Central system for state mortgage licensing
- Company (MU1) and individual (MU4) applications
- Uniform State Test (UST) for MLOs
- Continuing education requirements
- Background checks (FBI, state, credit)

### State-Specific Requirements
Most states require:
- State license in addition to federal registration
- Net worth and surety bond requirements
- Designated qualifying individual
- Branch office registrations
- Annual renewals and reporting

## Notable State Variations

### California
- Regulated by DFPI (Department of Financial Protection and Innovation)
- CFL license or CRA license depending on activity
- Specific disclosure requirements
- CCPA/CPRA privacy requirements

### New York
- Regulated by DFS (Department of Financial Services)
- Mortgage banker license required
- Strict cybersecurity requirements (23 NYCRR 500)
- Community Reinvestment Act considerations

### Texas
- Regulated by SML (Savings and Mortgage Lending)
- Home equity lending restrictions (Article XVI, Section 50)
- 80% LTV cap on home equity loans
- Specific cooling-off periods

### Florida
- Regulated by OFR (Office of Financial Regulation)
- Mortgage lender license required
- Net worth and liquid asset requirements
- MLO sponsorship requirements

### Illinois
- Regulated by IDFPR (Division of Financial Institutions)
- Residential Mortgage License Act
- Community Reinvestment Act-like requirements
- Specific advertising rules

## Common State-Level Requirements

### Disclosures
- High-cost loan disclosures (state thresholds may exceed federal)
- Prepayment penalty disclosures
- Rate lock disclosures
- Anti-steering disclosures

### Fee Restrictions
- Maximum points and fees (may be stricter than federal)
- Rate spread thresholds
- Prepayment penalty restrictions
- Late fee caps

### Servicing Requirements
- Loss mitigation requirements
- Foreclosure procedures and timelines
- Surplus funds distribution
- Communication requirements

## High-Cost Loan Triggers

Many states have "higher-priced" or "high-cost" loan thresholds:
- APR exceeds prime rate by certain percentage
- Points and fees exceed percentage of loan amount
- Additional disclosures and restrictions apply
- Some states have "covered" loan categories with extra protections

## Multi-State Operations Considerations

### Compliance Challenges
- Tracking 50+ sets of requirements
- State-specific forms and disclosures
- Varying licensing renewal dates
- Different examination schedules

### Best Practices
- Compliance management system (CMS)
- Regular regulatory monitoring
- State-by-state policy matrix
- Legal counsel in key states
- Industry association resources (MBA, MISMO)

## Recent Trends

### State-Level Privacy Laws
- California, Virginia, Colorado, Connecticut, Utah enacted comprehensive privacy laws
- More states considering similar legislation

### State Enforcement Actions
- Increased state AG involvement in mortgage matters
- Multi-state settlements for major violations
- Focus on fair lending and servicing

### Licensing Modernization
- Expanded NMLS functionality
- Reciprocity discussions
- Passporting concepts under consideration
